# The first runs, 1913–1924

A careful record of every paper known to have printed a crossword from the first one
(New York World, December 21, 1913) to the end of 1924, when the craze made them
everywhere. For each: the first date known, and **whose puzzle it was**. Most papers
before 1924 didn't make their own. They printed the World's, syndicated with its Sunday
FUN section, or a substitute. So "first crossword in paper X" and "first crossword
made by paper X" are different claims, and this page keeps them apart.

Each claim says where it comes from. Much of the 1914–1922 picture so far rests on one
researcher, T Campbell, whose "Year in Crosswords" series on Substack searches
digitized papers year by year ([1914](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1914-the-year-in-crosswords), [1915](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1915-the-year-in-crosswords), [1916](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1916-the-year-in-crosswords),
[1917](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1917-the-year-in-crosswords), [1920](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1920-the-year-in-crosswords), [1921](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1921-the-year-in-crosswords); he notes his searches can't be
complete). His posts were read here only through search-engine summaries (the pages
didn't load in this session), so each needs reading in full and checking against the
scans.

## What this shows so far

- **The World's puzzle was syndicated from the start**, or nearly: FUN, the World's
  Sunday supplement, was reprinted in other papers by February 1, 1914, when the
  Minneapolis Journal printed the World's No. 7, six weeks after the first. Those reprints are second
  copies of the World's puzzles, and since the World's FUN section survives badly
  ("Help wanted" in [new-york-world.md](publications/new-york-world.md)), they may be
  the best way to collect the World's 1913–1924 puzzles. Reprints ran days to weeks
  late, so a reprint's date is not the World's date.
- **The Journal's date is the World's only through 1914-05-10.** Nos. 7 to 21 ran one a
  week on the World's count. Then the Journal missed Sundays (05-17, 05-24, 06-07, 11-15)
  and skipped one number (No. 34), and ran two to three weeks behind. Every deviation
  from the weekly count seen so far is the Journal's, so the World's own dates still fit
  one puzzle every Sunday from 1913-12-21, inferred and not yet confirmed by a World page.
  The Journal's last printed number is No. 54 on 1915-01-17; no number appears on any
  later page or in any 1916–17 copy in any paper (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-27/29;
  [newyorkworld](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/newyorkworld) printings.tsv). See
  [minneapolis-journal.md](publications/minneapolis-journal.md).
- **Some papers ran a weekly crossword of unclear origin before 1920.** The Detroit Free
  Press had one by 1919-12-28. Whether it was the World's is the first thing to settle.
- **A "Cross-Word Puzzle Corner" ran in Omaha (by 1920-07-11) and Buffalo (by
  1920-10-31)**, under the same heading, with readers' puzzles credited by name and
  town (Lottie M. Arnold of Council Bluffs; Edgar W. Smith of Brooklyn). One
  syndicated feature, or a heading both papers borrowed? To settle.
- **"Jerry King's Cross-Word Puzzle" (1920–21) was the first rival series we know
  of.** For about two months in early 1920 the World's FUN section and its partners
  ran no new crosswords. Walter A. Walker registered a copyright through the Pittsburg
  Press that winter for "Jerry King's Crossword Puzzle" (Jerry King probably a
  pseudonym), and from mid-March the Press and the Minneapolis Journal ran it in the
  World puzzle's place; the Vancouver Sun took it too. It was still running in 1921
  (Campbell 1920, 1921). Alex's Pittsburgh Press copy of 1920-03-14 says the solution
  will appear next Sunday and prints no previous solution, so it is probably the
  first.
- **The Boston Globe's first crossword was the World's.** The Globe's puzzle of
  1917-02-25 (page 4, no credit) is the same puzzle the Tacoma Daily Ledger ran on
  1917-03-11 as "Fun's Cross-Word Puzzle": same grid, same hand-lettered numbers, same
  clues (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-27). Campbell was right. The Globe ran it two weeks
  before Tacoma and dropped the FUN name. When the Globe's own puzzles start is now
  open: by 1923-05-26, if the Saturday series was its own.
- **How the World's puzzles traveled.** The two copies differ only in typesetting
  ("clews" and "Mahometan" in the Globe, "clues" and "Mohammedan" in Tacoma), so the
  grid probably went out as a plate or mat and the clues as text each paper set itself.
  Partners ran the same puzzle on different Sundays (the Globe 14 days before Tacoma),
  so a reprint's date says little about the World's date.

- **The World's series is numbered, so reprints can be dated back to the World.** The
  World printed its series number in the grid (No. 7 on 1914-02-01, No. 8 on 1914-02-08,
  rising weekly to No. 21 on 1914-05-10, in the Minneapolis Journal; the numbering fades
  out after January 1915). That fits a puzzle every Sunday
  from No. 1 on 1913-12-21: World No. *n* ran on 1913-12-21 plus *n*−1 weeks. So a
  reprint whose number is legible can be tied to a World puzzle and an inferred World
  date, marked as inferred. This is how reprints in different papers (the Globe and
  Tacoma in 1917) could point to one puzzle without the World's own page.

- **Unnumbered puzzles in 1916–17 can be tied together by their constructors' credits and
  first clues.** H. H. Johnson's puzzle ("A Japanese musical instrument") ran in Tacoma on
  1917-02-18 and the LA Tribune on 1917-04-01, and Pittsburgh printed its solution on
  02-18 (so it ran there 02-11). William G. Melrose's ("A mouthful") ran in Pittsburgh on
  02-18 and Tacoma on 02-25. Miss Bertha L. Botjer's ("An aromatic plant") ran in
  Shreveport on 02-04, the day Pittsburgh printed its solution. So Tacoma ran a week behind
  Pittsburgh and the LA Tribune six weeks behind Tacoma. The LA Tribune's Christmas "Santa
  Claus" puzzle ran on 1917-02-25. All of these are printing dates, not World dates (Alex
  DeJarnatt, 2026-10-27/29; newyorkworld printings.tsv, `puzzle_key`).
- **FUN credited reader constructors**, by name and a New York address, from 1914
  (Katherine Reilly, 1914-04-05) to 1917 (see [new-york-world.md](publications/new-york-world.md)).
- **The credit changed in 1914.** The Journal's FUN page carried "Copyright, 1914, by the
  Du Puy Syndicate" until 1914-04-12 and "by the Press Publishing Co." from 04-19
  ([du-puy.md](syndicates/du-puy.md)). Whether the World took FUN's syndication in-house
  in April 1914 is open.

## Plan: the World's FUN run, 1914–1917

Agreed 2026-10-27. **Status 2026-10-29: days 1-2 done; 44 of 211 World numbers (Nos. 1 to
211, 1913-12-21 to 1917-12-30) have a copy, from 109 printings in eight papers.** The Journal's
run from No. 7 to No. 54 is in (Nos. 34, 38, 42, 53 wanted) with 1915-17 spot checks; the
sequential fill of 1916-17 is next. Tables and status page:
[newyorkworld](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/newyorkworld),
https://everypuzzleproject.github.io/newyorkworld/. Clip the World's FUN crosswords from the Minneapolis Journal, one per
Sunday, from No. 7 (1914-02-01) for as long as the Journal carries them and at least
through 1917: 205 Sundays, World Nos. 7 to 211 if none was skipped. The run gives a master
list of World puzzles with their numbers and dates, shows any skips, and lets each
reprint elsewhere (the Boston Globe's 1917 Sundays, Tacoma, Pittsburgh) be matched to
the World puzzle it is, by number while the grids carry one and by clues after.

- For each Sunday: Journal date and page, the World number if printed, the heading, the
  1-Across clue, the clipping link, and the solution (printed the next Sunday).
- Record a Sunday whose page is missing from the scans as missing, not as a skip.
- Where the Journal stops carrying FUN, continue with the Pittsburgh Press (from
  1916-06-11) or the Tacoma Daily Ledger.
- The data goes in its own repo (`newyorkworld`, modelled on `bostonglobe`), with a
  status table of every expected Sunday and its inferred World number. Newspapers.com
  allows about 30 high-resolution saves a day, so the run takes a week or more.

## Open questions

- Every FUN partner, and when each started and stopped. Campbell's searches give a
  start; Newspapers.com and Chronicling America searches for "cross-word puzzle" and
  FUN's other headings (by year) would extend it.
- Was the World's puzzle syndicated with the whole FUN section, or on its own? Under
  what credit ("Copyright, Press Publishing Co. (The New York World)"?)
- Who else, if anyone, made their own crossword before 1923? So far no paper other
  than the World is known to have made its own before the Globe's Saturday series
  (1923-05-26), if Campbell is right about 1917, and whether that series was the
  Globe's own is not established either.

  haven't been read yet.
