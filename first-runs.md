# The first runs, 1913–1924

A careful record of every paper known to have printed a crossword from the first one
(New York World, December 21, 1913) to the end of 1924, when the craze made them
everywhere. For each: the first date known, and **whose puzzle it was**. Most papers
before 1924 didn't make their own. They printed the World's, syndicated with its Sunday
FUN section, or a substitute. So "first crossword in paper X" and "first crossword
made by paper X" are different claims, and this page keeps them apart.

Each claim says where it comes from. Much of the 1914–1922 picture so far rests on one
researcher, T Campbell, whose "Year in Crosswords" series on Substack searches
digitized papers year by year ([1914](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1914-the-year-in-crosswords), [1915](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1915-the-year-in-crosswords), [1916](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1916-the-year-in-crosswords),
[1917](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1917-the-year-in-crosswords), [1920](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1920-the-year-in-crosswords), [1921](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1921-the-year-in-crosswords); he notes his searches can't be
complete). His posts were read here only through search-engine summaries (the pages
didn't load in this session), so each needs reading in full and checking against the
scans.

From October 1924 the syndicates join in: E&P's first Syndicate Directory (1924-10-25)
lists nine crossword features from eight syndicates (Bell, International, King, Ledger
(two), McClure, Metropolitan, the Herald Tribune, and the New York World Syndicate, so
by then the World was selling its puzzle formally), and a Metropolitan ad of
1924-10-18 names client papers: the St. Louis Post-Dispatch, the Washington Post, the
Cleveland Plain Dealer and others (see
[census/ep-crossword-features.md](census/ep-crossword-features.md)). The syndicates
are in [syndicates/](syndicates/).

Outside the US (secondary sources, unchecked): Pearson's Magazine, London, February
1922; the Sunday Express, 1924-11-02, the first British newspaper.

## What this shows so far

- **The World's puzzle was syndicated from the start**, or nearly: FUN, the World's
  Sunday supplement, was reprinted in other papers by February 1, 1914, when the
  Minneapolis Journal printed the World's "Cross-Word Puzzle No. 5", six weeks after
  the first. Its grid is also marked "No. 7": seven Sundays from December 21 to
  February 1 counting both ends, so the World may have numbered puzzles and Sundays
  differently. Those reprints are second
  copies of the World's puzzles, and since the World's FUN section survives badly
  ("Help wanted" in [new-york-world.md](publications/new-york-world.md)), they may be
  the best way to collect the World's 1913–1924 puzzles. Reprints ran days to weeks
  late, so a reprint's date is not the World's date.
- **Some papers ran a weekly crossword of unclear origin before 1920.** The Detroit Free
  Press had one by 1919-12-28. Whether it was the World's is the first thing to settle.
- **A "Cross-Word Puzzle Corner" ran in Omaha (by 1920-07-11) and Buffalo (by
  1920-10-31)**, under the same heading, with readers' puzzles credited by name and
  town (Lottie M. Arnold of Council Bluffs; Edgar W. Smith of Brooklyn). One
  syndicated feature, or a heading both papers borrowed? To settle.
- **"Jerry King's Cross-Word Puzzle" (1920–21) was the first rival series we know
  of.** For about two months in early 1920 the World's FUN section and its partners
  ran no new crosswords. Walter A. Walker registered a copyright through the Pittsburg
  Press that winter for "Jerry King's Crossword Puzzle" (Jerry King probably a
  pseudonym), and from mid-March the Press and the Minneapolis Journal ran it in the
  World puzzle's place; the Vancouver Sun took it too. It was still running in 1921
  (Campbell 1920, 1921). Alex's Pittsburgh Press copy of 1920-03-14 says the solution
  will appear next Sunday and prints no previous solution, so it is probably the
  first.
- **The Boston Globe's 1917 start is in question.** The catalog has the Globe's first
  crossword on 1917-02-25 (the Globe's own archive says 1917-03-04), and its 2022
  replay of 1917-08-19 calls it one of the Globe's first. Campbell says the Globe
  didn't make its own crosswords in 1917 but printed the World's FUN puzzles, like
  Tacoma and Pittsburgh. If so, the Globe's 1917 puzzles are World puzzles, and the
  Globe's own run may start later (by 1923-05-26 at the latest). The 1917-02-25 puzzle (page 4,
  Alex's clipping) carries no byline, number, copyright or FUN credit, but uses the
  World's start–end clue numbering and hand-lettered grid numbers. That neither proves
  nor rules out a World reprint. Check: the rest of page 4 and its section, and
  bg1917-02-25.xd against the World's puzzles (or Tacoma's or Pittsburgh's) of the same
  weeks.

## Open questions

- Every FUN partner, and when each started and stopped. Campbell's searches give a
  start; Newspapers.com and Chronicling America searches for "cross-word puzzle" and
  FUN's other headings (by year) would extend it.
- Was the World's puzzle syndicated with the whole FUN section, or on its own? Under
  what credit ("Copyright, Press Publishing Co. (The New York World)"?)
- Who else, if anyone, made their own crossword before 1923? So far no paper other
  than the World is known to have made its own before the Globe's Saturday series
  (1923-05-26), if Campbell is right about 1917, and whether that series was the
  Globe's own is not established either.
- The 1918–1919 and 1922–1923 posts in Campbell's series (1922 is in three parts)
  haven't been read yet.
