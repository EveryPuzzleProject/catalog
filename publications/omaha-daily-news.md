"The Cross-Word Puzzle Corner", in the paper's Sunday magazine, printed readers'
puzzles. On July 11, 1920: "Lottie M. Arnold, Council Bluffs, Ia., is the author of this
Cross-Word Puzzle. The solution will be given in next Sunday's Omaha Daily News
Magazine." Her grid is built of separate sections with their own clue lists (Top, Left
Side, Bottom, Right Side) around a central Horizontal/Vertical part, numbered by start
and end square (1-4 A narrative; vertical 2-9 An indefinite article) (clipping from
Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-27).

The Buffalo Courier used the same heading, "Cross-Word Puzzle Corner", on October 31,
1920, for a puzzle "by Edgar W. Smith of Brooklyn, N. Y." (see
[buffalo-courier.md](buffalo-courier.md)). Two papers far apart, the same heading,
readers' puzzles credited by name and town, World-style numbering: that suggests a
shared source, perhaps a syndicated "Corner" collecting readers' puzzles, rather than
each paper's own. Council Bluffs is across the river from Omaha, though, so Arnold
could equally be a local reader. Not established.

Open questions: when the Corner began and ended; whether it was the paper's own or a
syndicated feature shared with Buffalo (look for a copyright line, and for the same
puzzles in other papers); whether Omaha printed puzzles by readers outside the area.
