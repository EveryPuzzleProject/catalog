The first crossword ran on Sunday, September 14, 1924, in the Woman's Section. A
crossword was new enough to come with directions on how to solve one, and a short
introduction calling "cross-word-puzzle-itis" the newest epidemic. The daily puzzle
followed on Monday, December 15, 1924, again with instructions, answered the next day.
By February 1925 the
Tribune was running readers' puzzles as a paid contest, with the maker's name and
address under the grid.

Open: whether the Sunday puzzle ran every week between September and December 1924, how long the reader contest lasted, and when the
Tribune switched to syndicated puzzles.
