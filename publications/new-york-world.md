The first crossword, Arthur Wynne's "Word-Cross Puzzle", ran here on December
21, 1913. The World closed in 1931.

**The World's puzzles were syndicated almost from the start.** Other papers reprinted
the Sunday FUN section, crossword and all: the Minneapolis Journal by February 1,
1914 (its first: the World's "Cross-Word Puzzle No. 5", six weeks after the first; the grid
is also marked "No. 7", meaning unknown), the
Pittsburgh Press from June 11, 1916, the Fort Wayne Journal-Gazette and the Tacoma Daily
Ledger in 1916, the Salt Lake Telegram in 1917, and the Boston Globe in 1917 (its first
crossword, 1917-02-25, is the World's: Tacoma ran the same puzzle on 1917-03-11 as "Fun's
Cross-Word Puzzle") ([Campbell, 1915](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1915-the-year-in-crosswords),
[1916](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1916-the-year-in-crosswords),
[1917](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1917-the-year-in-crosswords)). Alex DeJarnatt found the
Journal's 1914-02-01 reprint. The reprints ran days to weeks after the World
(Tacoma printed a Christmas puzzle on January 7), so their dates aren't the World's.
Wherever the World's own pages are missing, a reprint is a second copy of the puzzle.
By the first E&P Syndicate Directory (October 1924) the New York World Syndicate lists
its "Cross Word Puzzle (with solution)" by "World Staff" (see
[syndicates/new-york-world.md](../syndicates/new-york-world.md)).

For about two months in early 1920 the FUN section and its partners ran no new
crosswords, and some partners switched to "Jerry King's Cross-Word Puzzle" from the
Pittsburgh Press (see [pittsburgh-press.md](pittsburgh-press.md)).
