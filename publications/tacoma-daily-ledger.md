Reprinted the New York World's Sunday FUN crosswords from 1916, under the heading
"Fun's Cross-Word Puzzle", which names the source in a way the Boston Globe's copies
don't.

**It settled the Boston Globe's first crossword.** Tacoma's puzzle of March 11, 1917 is
the Globe's of February 25, 1917: identical grid, identical hand-lettered numbers, and
the same clues. So the Globe's first crossword was the World's (see
[boston-globe.md](boston-globe.md)). Tacoma ran it two weeks after the Globe.

The two copies differ only in typesetting: the Globe has "clews" and "Mahometan
religion", Tacoma "clues" and "Mohammedan religion"; the Globe adds "The solution of
this cross-word puzzle will be printed next Sunday." The grids match down to the
lettering, so the grid seems to have gone out as a ready-made printing plate or mat,
and the clues as text each paper set in its own type. That's an inference from two
copies.

**Tacoma ran one week behind Pittsburgh** in February 1917: Johnson's puzzle on 02-18
(Pittsburgh 02-11, inferred) and Melrose's on 02-25 (Pittsburgh 02-18), both credited to
New York-area readers by name and address. On 02-18 Tacoma printed its own puzzle's
solution a week early, as "last week's" (Alex DeJarnatt; newyorkworld printings.tsv).

Open questions: Tacoma's first and last FUN puzzles; how far behind the World it ran
(Campbell's Christmas puzzle on January 7 suggests about two weeks, the same gap as
here, behind the Globe); whether it ever printed the World's credit beyond "Fun's".
