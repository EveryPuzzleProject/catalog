Mike Shenk graduated from high school in 1975, took a mathematics degree at
Penn State in 1979 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Shenk) and later edited
the Wall Street Journal crossword. Per Penn State's profile
(https://www.psu.edu/news/arts-and-entertainment/story/enigmatic-career-math-alum-wall-street-journals-puzzle-guru)
he was the first to construct daily crosswords for the Collegian, hired after
he sent samples to the new editor-in-chief at the end of his freshman year.
Will Shortz is quoted there saying college-paper crosswords are usually weekly
or less, and Shenk is the only person he knows to have done it daily. Wikipedia
says he sometimes credited constructions to anagram pseudonyms, one of them
"Collegian".

The archive is listed in https://libguides.fau.edu/historical-us-newspapers/p
(1887–2010; predecessors Free Lance 1887–1904, State Collegian 1904–1911, Penn
State Collegian 1911–1940).

Alex DeJarnatt searched the archive on 2026-10-02. The earliest puzzle with
Shenk's byline is March 12, 1976, and an unbylined puzzle ran March 11, 1976,
probably his with the byline left off. Wikipedia's mention of the anagram
pseudonym "Collegian" may be relevant to unbylined or oddly bylined puzzles.
Spot checks of years before 1976 found no crossword, which fits the profile's
claim that he was the first. The archive's scans do include puzzle pages for
this period.

On June 10, 1981 a help-wanted ad ran where the puzzle had been, saying Shenk
had secured a job with GAMES magazine. So his run lasted about five years,
1976–1981, past his 1979 graduation. The Penn State profile's "three years"
is too short.

The latest Shenk puzzle found is May 15, 1981, possibly his last.

The archive has a big gap around May and June 1981, so the issues between
May 15 and June 10 may not be scanned. The puzzle also doesn't appear in every
issue that is scanned: not completely daily (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-02).

Open questions: whether May 15, 1981 was really his last puzzle (may not be
answerable from the scans because of the gap); how many of his puzzles actually
ran, e.g. a count for one full school year;
whether the ad found a successor, and what ran after; whether the archive is
free to everyone or needs a Penn State login.
