Best-documented college paper so far. Source for the history is the Daily's
own 2023 feature
(https://stanforddaily.com/2023/02/23/word-nerd-nation-the-surprising-history-of-the-daily-crossword/):

- The Daily announced its first crossword on April 2, 1935; the first run
  lasted about two months.
- United Feature Syndicate supplied puzzles from the late 1930s.
- The Daily "announced the crossword's return in 1978"; it later stopped
  running the New York Times crossword in 2003 because of cost.
- Tribune Media Services supplied puzzles for an 11-year stretch in the early
  2000s; readers were unhappy.
- Student crossword editors began in 2013 (Ryan Smith '17). In 2013 the
  answers moved online while puzzles stayed in print only. A digital
  interactive crossword was launched in 2021 by Matthew Turk '24 for The Daily
  Magazine.
- The article also says the satirical magazine The Chaparral ran a Stanford
  crossword in 1925 (a different publication).

Open questions: which syndicate or service ran in each gap (1938–1978), the
exact years of the Tribune stretch, current frequency, and whether the archive
site's full-text search finds the puzzles themselves (grids may be scanned
as page images only). The archive page says registration is only needed to
correct OCR text.
