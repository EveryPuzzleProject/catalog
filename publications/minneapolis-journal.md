Reprinted the New York World's crosswords almost from the start. On February 1, 1914,
six weeks after the World's first crossword (1913-12-21), the Journal printed
"Cross-Word Puzzle No. 5": "Today the fifth puzzle in the series is presented." A clue
reads "What FUN is printed on", so it came with, or from, the World's Sunday FUN
section (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-27). This is the earliest reprint of a
World crossword known so far.

**Two numbers.** The Journal's heading says No. 5, but the grid itself, in its center
block, says "No. 7". The grid came from the World, so "No. 7" is most likely the World's
number, and "No. 5" the Journal's own count. That means the Journal joined two puzzles
into the World's series: its No. 1 was the World's No. 3.

If the World ran one puzzle a week from December 21 with no gaps, its No. 7 fell on
February 1, 1914, the same day the Journal printed it, and its No. 3 on January 4. So
the Journal may have run the World's puzzles on the World's own dates, with no delay,
unlike the later reprints (Tacoma's ran weeks behind). That rests on the World never
skipping a week, which is not checked: compare with the World's own dates.

These reprints are a second copy of the World's earliest puzzles, which survive badly
in the World itself. The Journal's Nos. 1 to 4 (the World's Nos. 3 to 6, January 1914) would be second
copies of some of the first crosswords ever printed.

In mid-March 1920, when the World's FUN section had gone two months without new
crosswords, the Journal and the Pittsburgh Press began running "Jerry King's Cross-Word
Puzzle" instead (see [pittsburgh-press.md](pittsburgh-press.md)).

Open questions: the Journal's Nos. 1 to 4 (from about January 4, 1914), and whether
its No. 1 says "No. 3" in the grid; whether the World's grids all carry their number
like this; the World's dates for Nos. 3 to 7 (did the Journal run them the same day?);
the last FUN reprint; whether the reprints carry the World's credit; whether
the Journal ever made its own.
