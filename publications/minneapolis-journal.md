Reprinted the New York World's crosswords almost from the start. Its first FUN
crossword ran on February 1, 1914, six weeks after the World's first (1913-12-21):
the World's puzzle No. 7 (headed "Cross-Word Puzzle No. 5" by the Journal), with a clue
"What FUN is printed on" (Alex DeJarnatt,
Newspapers.com, 2026-10-27; Alex checked that no earlier Journal issue has a FUN
puzzle). This is the earliest reprint of a World crossword known so far.

**The number is the grid's, not the heading's.** The first puzzle's heading says "No. 5"
and its text "Today the fifth puzzle in the series is presented", but the grid's center
block says "No. 7". The next Sunday (1914-02-08, "Can You Solve Them?") the grid says
"No. 8" and the Journal gives no number of its own: it dropped its wrong count and went
with the World's (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-27). The numbers rise one a week after that
to No. 21 on 1914-05-10 (see below). So the grid number is the World's series number, and "No. 5" was the
Journal's mistake.

**Dating the World's puzzles from it (inferred, not printed).** World No. 7 on
1914-02-01 and No. 8 on 1914-02-08 fit a puzzle every Sunday from No. 1 on 1913-12-21:
World No. *n* ran on 1913-12-21 plus *n*−1 weeks. On that count the Journal printed each
puzzle on the World's own date through No. 21 on 1914-05-10. Both points rest on the
World never skipping a Sunday between December 21 and February 1, which a World page would
confirm. Until then, any World date worked out this way is an inference, shown as one,
with this reasoning.

## The run week by week, 1914

Newspapers.com pages, Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-27/29; one row per page in
[newyorkworld printings.tsv](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/newyorkworld), which
holds the detail; this is the shape.

- **No. 7 (1914-02-01) to No. 21 (05-10): one a week**, on the World's count. Nos. 10, 13
  and 18 print no number; each is inferred from the page carrying the solution to the
  previous number (No. 9, 12, 17), marked as inferred in the table's `match`.
- **Then the Journal fell behind.** It printed no new FUN puzzle on 05-17, 05-24 (found by
  text search; not yet confirmed by browsing the pages) and 06-07 (the page carries only
  No. 22's solution). No. 22 ran on 05-31, No. 23 on 06-14: three weeks behind the
  inferred World date by late June.
- **No. 33 on 08-23, then No. 35 on 08-30.** No. 34 never ran here: the Journal dropped
  one number and was two weeks behind from then.
- **11-15 was missed** (that page has only the last solution), back to three weeks.
  No. 45 on 11-08 is the last number printed in 1914; Nos. 46–52 (11-22 to 1915-01-03),
  and earlier Nos. 31 (08-09) and 44 (11-01), are inferred by sequence from the
  neighbouring printed numbers. **No. 54 on 1915-01-17 is the last number printed** (verified on
  the page). No number appears on any 1915 page after that, nor in any 1916–17 copy in any
  paper (newyorkworld printings.tsv).

**What this shows (carefully).** Every deviation from the weekly count seen so far is
the Journal's: Sundays it missed, one number it skipped. Nothing seen so far suggests the
World itself skipped a week, so the World's dates still fit one puzzle every Sunday from
1913-12-21. But the Journal's date is the World's only through 1914-05-10.

**Where the number is printed moved**: the grid's center block in early 1914; FUN's
heading or large type beside the grid from May, as the grids became irregular shapes (an
X, an eagle, a W, a letter N, a car, a double diamond).

**Credit.** The puzzle page's banner carries "(Copyright, 1914, by the Du Puy
Syndicate.)" from 02-15 through 04-12 and "(Copyright, 1914, by the Press Publishing
Co.)" (the World's publisher) from 04-19 through August; none from September. The banner
reads "FUN and PUZZLES" from October 1914 and "Puzzles and Humor" in 1915. See
[du-puy.md](../syndicates/du-puy.md). The solution printed 1914-12-13 (to the 12-06
puzzle, No. 48 inferred) spells NEW YORK WORLD and SUNDAY FUN across its middle row.

## 1915 and after

FUN's crossword ran here every week we have a page for from 1915-01-24 to 07-25. The
Journal then carried none from 08-01 to 11-14 (a puzzle corner of its own instead;
newyorkworld searches.tsv), and FUN's crossword returns on 1915-11-21 with no
previous-week solution. Copies continue to 1916-12-03. Nine spot checks in 1917 found no
Journal page (searches.tsv, no-hit, not browsed).

The design was a template: the same diamond with a shaded cross in the middle, the
puzzle number printed in the cross.

These numbers could also identify the World's puzzles independently of any one
printing ("World No. 7"), so reprints in different papers (the Journal, the Globe,
Tacoma) can point to the same puzzle even where the World's own page is missing. See
[first-runs.md](../first-runs.md).

These reprints are a second copy of the World's early puzzles, which survive badly in
the World itself.

In mid-March 1920, when the World's FUN section had gone two months without new
crosswords, the Journal and the Pittsburgh Press began running "Jerry King's Cross-Word
Puzzle" instead (see [pittsburgh-press.md](pittsburgh-press.md)).

Open questions: whether the World skipped any Sunday before No. 7 (a World page for
December 1913 or January 1914, or another partner's, would confirm the count); whether the
World kept numbering its grids after January 1915 (the Journal stopped printing the number
then); why the Journal fell behind in May 1914; the last FUN reprint; whether the Journal
ever made its own; what the 1915 puzzle corner was.
