Printed as *The Pittsburg Press* in these years. Arthur Wynne, who made the World's first
crossword, had worked at the Press before moving to New York.

Two different series before 1924:

- **The World's puzzles, from 1916-06-11.** The Press carried the New York World's
  Sunday FUN section and its crossword (T Campbell). These are second copies of World
  puzzles, printed later than in the World.
- **"Jerry King's Cross-Word Puzzle", from 1920-03-14.** For about two months in early
  1920 the World's FUN section and its partners had no new crosswords. Walter A. Walker
  registered a copyright that winter, through the Press, for "Jerry King's Crossword
  Puzzle" ("Jerry King" probably a pseudonym), and from mid-March the Press and the
  Minneapolis Journal ran it in the World puzzle's place; the Vancouver Sun took it
  too, and it was still running in 1921 ([Campbell 1920](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1920-the-year-in-crosswords),
  [1921](https://tcampbell.substack.com/p/1921-the-year-in-crosswords)). Alex DeJarnatt's copy of 1920-03-14 promises the solution next
  Sunday and has none for an earlier puzzle, which fits it being the first.

**Its 1917 copies are the World's, from the same plate.** The Press ran FUN's crossword
on 1916-10-01, 1917-02-04 and 02-18 (Alex DeJarnatt; newyorkworld printings.tsv). On
1917-02-04 the page carries "Copyright, 1917, by The Press Pub. Co." on another FUN
feature, Bobby in Alphabet Land: the Press Publishing Co. is the World's publisher, so the
page came from the World. The Press ran Johnson's puzzle (02-11, inferred from the
solution it printed 02-18) and Botjer's (01-28, inferred from the solution printed 02-04)
a week before Tacoma and Shreveport. Its start, 1916-06-11, is still Campbell's, not yet
checked against a page.

So Jerry King's puzzle was the Press's own property (the copyright went through it),
and it sold it to other papers: the first rival to the World's that we know of. See
[syndicates/jerry-king.md](../syndicates/jerry-king.md) and
[first-runs.md](../first-runs.md).

Open questions: how long Jerry King's ran, and how many puzzles; who Walter A. Walker
was; whether the Press went back to the World's puzzles; when the Press's FUN reprints
began and ended exactly; the Press's crosswords after 1921.
