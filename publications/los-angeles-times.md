The common claim that the LA Times started its crossword in 1942 is about
the Times's own puzzle. It printed syndicated crosswords from 1924, starting
with Bell's daily. E&P's directories list Bell's crossword as twice a week in
October 1924 and "daily and Sunday" by July 1925
(sim_editor-publisher_1924-10-25_57_22, sim_editor-publisher_1925-07-18_58_8).

Bell's daily was still running in the Times on January 1, 1940, as "Today's
Crossword Puzzle", again with Saturday's answer on Monday (Alex DeJarnatt,
Newspapers.com, 2026-10-02). Whether it ran continuously from 1924 is not
known. Daily Bell puzzles kept running in the Times until at least July 15,
1963, the last one found (same source), so the 1942 start of the Times's own
crossword did not replace the Bell daily. Bell merged with McClure about
then: E&P lists "Bell Syndicate" in July 1962 and "Bell-McClure Syndicate"
in July 1963 (sim_editor-publisher_1962-07-28_95_30,
sim_editor-publisher_1963-07-27_96_30), and Bell-McClure still offered a
daily crossword, so the merger may not explain the end. In the 1963 Times,
other features such as comics are labeled "Bell-McClure Syndicate" while the
puzzle still says "Bell Syndicate" (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-02). Possible
reasons, none confirmed: the puzzles were made earlier (a backlog or reruns),
the puzzle's copyright line was never updated, or the crossword was kept under
the old name.
By December 25, 1963 the Times's daily puzzle is copyright General Features
Corporation. The first GFC puzzle found is January 13, 1962, so Bell and
General Features overlapped for about 18 months, until the last Bell puzzle
found on July 15, 1963 (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-02). During
the overlap the Times ran one or the other each day, not both, with no
apparent pattern (same source). So the overlap adds no extra puzzles to the
count, but which feature ran on a given day has to be checked day by day. In July 1963 E&P
lists GFC's "Daily Crossword" by Margaret Farrar (daily except Sunday) and a
Sunday "Crossword" by Maynard Nichols. This is the GFC puzzle the Los Angeles
Times Syndicate later sold as the "Los Angeles Times Crossword" (see below),
so the Times was printing it about ten years before taking it over.
Alex DeJarnatt's working guess is that the Times's own 1942 puzzle was a
Sunday feature, alongside the weekday Bell puzzle; not confirmed yet.

Two different things ran under the LA Times name:

- **The Sunday magazine, 1924.** An ad on Sunday, October 26, 1924 promised
  "a cross-words puzzle" in the next issue, and the Sunday magazine of
  November 2, 1924 has "How to solve the cross-word puzzle", with no byline
  (Alex DeJarnatt, from the Newspapers.com scans, 2026-10-02). This is the
  earliest known Times crossword.
- **A daily puzzle from late 1924.** On December 2, 1924 the paper (then
  titled the *Los Angeles Daily Times*) ran a "Daily Crossword Puzzle" with
  "Yesterday's Solution". The December 1 (Monday) puzzle is also a "Daily
  Crossword Puzzle", copyright Bell Syndicate (the line is printed "1921",
  presumably a misprint for 1924), and prints "Saturday's Solution", so a
  puzzle ran on Saturday, November 29 too, and apparently none on Sunday
  (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-02). The daily is therefore Bell's,
  like the November 27 puzzle in gxd.
  The earliest "Daily Crossword Puzzle" found on Newspapers.com is Saturday,
  November 29, 1924 (no copyright line), which prints "Solution to yesterday's
  puzzle", so one ran on Friday, November 28. With the gxd puzzle of Thursday,
  November 27, that suggests the daily began around Thanksgiving week, though
  November 27 and 28 haven't been found in the Newspapers.com scans yet.
  The November 28 puzzle's 1-Across answer was RIOT (from the solution printed
  November 29); the gxd November 27 puzzle's 1-Across is GROGGY, so they are
  different puzzles, as expected for consecutive days.
- **Syndicated puzzles printed by the Times.** The earliest known is a Bell
  Syndicate "Cross Word Puzzle" on November 27, 1924, which is in gxd. Its
  copyright line reads "The Ball Syndicate" (sic).
- **The Times's own crossword.** Per Alex DeJarnatt (2026-10-02): the Times ran
  a crossword in 1942, possibly skipped some years, and ran one regularly from
  the 1950s. It was syndicated to other papers in the 1960s and 1970s. The
  specific sources are still to be recorded.

**Syndication, from Editor & Publisher's Syndicate Directories**
(`census/ep-crossword-features.csv`, archive.org items as cited there):

- 1949 on: General Features Corporation (GFC) lists a crossword, by Maynard
  Nichols from 1950; from 1954 it also lists a "Daily Crossword" by Margaret
  Farrar. By 1973 the daily and Sunday puzzles credit Farrar and Betty Nichols.
- 1973–74: the Los Angeles Times Syndicate advertises the same Farrar–Nichols
  daily and Sunday puzzles as "formerly General Features", jointly with GFC
  (sim_editor-publisher_1974-07-27_107_30).
- 1975–80: listed as "Los Angeles Times Cross Words" / "Los Angeles Times
  Crossword Puzzle" by Margaret Farrar, daily and Sunday
  (sim_editor-publisher_1975-07-26_108_30; 1976 ad: "classic Los Angeles Times
  Crossword by legendary Margaret Farrar").
- 1985–2000: "Los Angeles Times Daily / Sunday Crossword Puzzle", by "Staff".

So the syndicated "LA Times crossword" of the 1970s seems to have been the
General Features Farrar puzzle under the Times's name after the Times's
syndicate took GFC over. Whether that was also the puzzle printed in the Times
itself is not known yet.
