# Leads

own. Move a lead to its own file once something ties the person to puzzles.

## The Cross Word Puzzle Book, Second and Third Series (Simon and Schuster, 1924)

Web search only so far (2026-10-13); no newspaper archives yet.

- **Jack Barrett** (ssb-97): a Jack Barrett made Scientific American's first two
  crosswords (February and March 1925) and set The Nation's cryptic from 1943 until his
  death about 1947. Common name; a Jack Barrett also edited a 1925 London crossword
  anthology. [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/anecdotes-from-the-archive/happy-100th-birthday-to-the-crossword/),
  [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/five-vie-puzzlers-mantle/tnamp/)
- **Porter R. Lee** (ssb-95): Porter Raymond Lee (born 1879-12-21, Buffalo; died
  1939-03-08) directed the New York
  School of Social Work 1917–38. Right place and time; nothing ties him to puzzles.
  [VCU Social Welfare History](https://socialwelfare.library.vcu.edu/people/lee-porter-r/)
- **Joseph A. Margolies** (ssb-52, 73, 119): the book calls him "Mr. Margolies of
  Brentano's" (the New York bookstore). Perhaps the Joseph A. Margolies who edited
  *Strange and Fantastic Stories* (1946).
- **Robert S. Holzman** (ssb-80): a tax author of that name was born in 1907. Nothing
  else fits yet.
- **Ruth von Phul** (not a byline): the Third Series opens with her "Ballade of Cross
  Words", signed as world crossword champion. Lived 1904–1986 (library authority
  record; exact dates not found). She won the 1925 Herald Tribune tournament
  over William A. Stern II and reportedly became a James Joyce scholar.
- **William A. Stern II** (with his wife, ssb-25): won the first crossword championship,
  New York, May 1924 (the Second Series quotes his time on No. 51).
- **Not the famous namesakes:** H. Emerson Hough (ssb-110) isn't the novelist Emerson
  Hough, who died in 1923; Arthur J. Goldberg (ssb-99) isn't the Supreme Court justice,
  then 16 and in Chicago.
- **Distinctive names, nothing found yet** (try 1920 and 1925 censuses and 1924–25 New
  York papers): John Boyajy (ssb-69), Irvan Neckerman (book 1 and five in books 2–3),
  John B. Sirich, Jr. (ssb-57), Gertrude B. Chestresmith (ssb-138), Madeleine Lubetty
  (ssb-116), Hugo Kladeviko (ssb-102), Leonard J. Brightman (ssb-107), Alfred A. Wicklund
  (ssb-121), John Fasel (ssb-59, 120), Harry R. Torr (ssb-124), Edith S. Iseman (ssb-98),
  Mordecai M. Richter (ssb-62), Wilmer H. Koch (ssb-91), Albert Buran (ssb-103).

## Judge (1924–1937)

From the byline check after the Judge export to gxd (2026-10-13). Bylines are as printed;
each was read against the scan.

- **Oscar F. Barrezueta** (Ecuador): sent puzzles over five years, so the bylines
  vary. "Oscar Barrezueta, Guayaquil, Ecuador" (judge1932-08-01); "Oscar F. Barrezueta
  V, Quito, Ecuador, South America" (judge1933-08-01); "Oscar F. Barrezueta, Quito,
  Ecuador, S. A." (judge1934-08-01b, judge1934-09-01, judge1935-04-01, judge1937-02-01).
  The "V" is probably the initial of his second surname (his mother's), as Spanish
  names often have; it is printed without a period. Treat the three forms as one
  person.
- **Omer. A. Dynes** (judge1925-11-28): printed with the period after "Omer", which is
  probably a printer's slip. Omer is an ordinary given name of the time, especially
  in the Midwest; try the 1920 and 1930 censuses for Indianapolis.
- **Woodward Woodward** (judge1931-06-06, Oak Park, Ill.; judge1931-12-26, Defiance,
  Ohio): printed clearly both times, from two towns. It may be a real name or a pen name;
  nothing found yet. Possibly tied to the Ridgefield Woodward bylines and to
  [Margaret Shane](margaret-shane.md), who may have been born a Woodward.
