**Evidence.** The surname is very rare, and crossword-history write-ups say the Times's
first constructor had been publishing puzzles since 1924. He had eight NYT puzzles, and
others in the Washington Post, the Herald Tribune and Simon and Schuster books. No. 67
("A Staid Form") notes that "this odd pattern was used by another contributor and will
be found elsewhere in the book."

**Open.** The Pre-Shortzian Puzzle Project's biography (written with his grand-nephew,
Donald Erlenkotter, UCLA professor emeritus) wasn't reachable when this was written:
read it for dates and life details. One snippet said he was 73 at his NYT debut; check
before repeating it. Find the other S&S puzzle with No. 67's pattern. Donald Erlenkotter
is the person to ask whether the family knew of a 1924 puzzle.
