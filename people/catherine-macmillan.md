**Judge.** Judge printed 82 of her crosswords, from No. 6 (1927-06-04) to No. 386
(August 1937): about a fifth of all the puzzles it printed in those years, and four
times as many as its next most frequent contributor (T. H. Merriman, 22). In 1930 she
had 19 of the year's 52. Every byline gives Chicago; two in 1929 give 4507 Lake Park
Ave., in Kenwood. Some Judge bylines print "Chicago, Ill" without the period, and
judge1930-08-30 reads "Chicago IU." in gxd, probably an OCR slip for "Ill."; they are
listed together above. Two bylines spell her name McMillan.

Her clues are longer and more often jokes than other contributors' in every year she
appears. They return to Chicago in the Capone years ("What kind of people form a
considerable part of the population of Chicago?" MURDERERS; "This makes a daily report
in Chicago" GUN), to stingy-Scotsman jokes ("This will even make a Scotchman give"
CHRISTMAS), to crosswords themselves ("What girl often helps in constructing Crossword
Puzzles?" ADA; "What puzzle makers often become" BATTY), and to Judge ("This will bring
'Judge' for ten weeks" DOLLAR).

**The Chicago Tribune.** The same name and address appear in the Tribune twice, which
is what ties these items to her:

- 1925-02-26: among the prize winners of the Tribune's crossword contest, which paid $20
  for each puzzle accepted; the OCR reads "Catherine MacMillan … 4507 … Avenue;
  Chicago", with her grid printed. Her earliest puzzle found so far, two years before
  Judge.
- 1941-04-12: a winner in the Tribune's "Our Flag" crossword contest (a solving
  contest), "Catherine MacMillan, 4507 Lake Park, Chicago".

Likely hers too: a light verse, "HE", signed "CATHERINE MacMILLAN" in the Tribune's "In
the Wake of the News" column (1929-09-20), a letter-block joke finding HE in He-aven,
He-l and He-art; and an alliteration contest entry "submitted by Catherine MacMillan"
(1943-04-17; the OCR doesn't show her town).

4507 Lake Park was a multi-flat apartment building; Tribune rental ads list flats there
in 1917 and 1938.

**Life.** The 1930 census lists her at 4507 Lake Park Ave., Chicago (Ward 4): Catherine
MacMillan, 41, born in Wisconsin, single, a public school teacher, living with two
sisters, 46 and 52, also single and also public school teachers. The address is the one
in her Judge bylines and in both Tribune contests (1925, 1941), so she lived there from
at least 1925 to 1941. Censuses list two other Catherine MacMillans in Chicago then
(Catherine R., born about 1907 in North Dakota; Catherine F., born about 1913 in
Illinois); the address rules them out.

**Open.** Her exact birth and death dates, her sisters' names, and where she taught. The
1940 census (with its question on residence in 1935) and the Chicago Board of Education's
lists of teachers may help; an obituary would give the rest. A Tribune archive search (1924–1950) may
find an obituary or more verse. No puzzles under her name are in gxd outside Judge,
including the NYT from 1942, though many NYT puzzles of the 1940s–70s have no known
author. Judge's Cross Word Puzzle Books (a fourth, fifth and sixth were advertised) may
reprint her puzzles. The Tribune's 1925 contest page should be read against the scan
(her puzzle and the address), and the page links above checked.
