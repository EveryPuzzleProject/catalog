Two Scripps syndicates, merged in May 1978 as United Media Enterprises
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Media)). NEA served smaller
papers with a full package of features; United Feature sold to bigger ones. Both sold
crosswords for most of the century, mostly unsigned ("Staff").

The Stanford Daily ran United Feature crosswords from the late 1930s
([stanford-daily.md](../publications/stanford-daily.md)).

From June 1, 2011 Universal Uclick (now Andrews McMeel Syndication) ran syndication for
United Media's roughly 150 features, NEA's included, while Scripps kept the copyrights
([Scripps press release](https://scripps.com/press-releases/771-universal-uclick-to-provide-syndicate-services-for-united-media/)).
So a United Media crossword after 2011 may be sold by AMU but copyright Scripps, or a
later owner: a case where the rights holder and the distributor differ.

Open questions: whether the NEA and United Feature crosswords were among the features
that went to Universal Uclick, and what they're called now; who made the unsigned
puzzles; copyright lines as printed ("© NEA", "© United Feature Syndicate").
