Hearst's syndicate, and the only one in every E&P directory from the first (1924) to
2000. Its two long-running crosswords carry names that read like bylines but may not be:
"Eugene Sheffer" from 1934 and "Thomas Joseph" from 1972. Whether either name stayed
on the puzzle after the person stopped making it is worth checking before treating
the byline as a constructor (see [people/](../people/)). "Thomas Joseph" in particular
needs looking into: a real person, or a house name?

Central Press Association was founded in Cleveland in 1910 by V. V. McNitt, became part
of King Features in 1930, and served smaller papers until 1971
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Press_Association)). Its "CP Crossword
Puzzle" is in E&P from 1925 (before King owned it) to 1971.

Open questions: whether King still sells these crosswords (the E&P census stops at
2000); who made the Sheffer and Joseph puzzles in later years; copyright lines as
printed ("© King Features Syndicate, Inc."?).
