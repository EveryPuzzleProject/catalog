The clearest case of an editor who was his own syndicate. Reagle started with the San
Francisco Examiner's new Sunday magazine in 1985 and went into syndication about three
years later, selling to papers one at a time until 50 to 60 ran his Sunday puzzle,
among them the Washington Post, the Los Angeles Times, the Philadelphia Inquirer and the
Seattle Times. He kept all rights to his puzzles, and in an NPR interview called his
the only self-syndicated Sunday puzzle in the country. He died on August 22, 2015.

Here rights holder, distributor and constructor are the same person, so the copyright
line presumably names Reagle himself (to check in print).

He is **not in E&P's Syndicate Directory** in any year we extracted (1988–2000), though
he was syndicating to dozens of papers. Either self-syndicators didn't list
themselves, or he didn't. That matters for using E&P as a census: the self-syndicated
category may be missing from it.
