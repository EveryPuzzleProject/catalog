The first crossword syndication, from the first weeks: other papers reprinted the World's Sunday FUN section,
crossword and all. Known partners so far (T Campbell's posts, read through search
summaries; to check): the Minneapolis Journal (from 1914-02-01, the World's No. 7, found by Alex
DeJarnatt), the Pittsburgh Press (from
1916-06-11), the Fort Wayne Journal-Gazette and the Tacoma Daily Ledger (1916), the Salt
Lake Telegram (1917), and the Boston Globe (1917, without credit: its 1917-02-25
puzzle is the one Tacoma ran on 1917-03-11 as "Fun's Cross-Word Puzzle"). The reprints ran
days to weeks after the World. The full list is kept in
[first-runs.md](../first-runs.md).

For about two months in early 1920 FUN had no new crosswords; the Pittsburgh Press's
"Jerry King" puzzle took its place in some papers (see [jerry-king.md](jerry-king.md)).

By October 1924 the New York World Syndicate is in E&P's first directory with a "Cross
Word Puzzle (with solution)" by "World Staff". The World closed in 1931.

**The credit changed in April 1914.** The Journal's puzzle page read "(Copyright, 1914, by
the Du Puy Syndicate.)" from 02-15 to 04-12 and "by the Press Publishing Co." (the
World's publisher) from 04-19 through August; none after. See [du-puy.md](du-puy.md).
Open: did the World take FUN's syndication in-house in April 1914? The 1914-12-13 solution
spells NEW YORK WORLD / SUNDAY FUN across its middle row, a credit built into the grid.

Open questions: whether the crossword was sold with the whole FUN section or on its
own, and under what credit; every partner and its dates; how the World Syndicate's 1924
listing relates to the FUN reprints.
